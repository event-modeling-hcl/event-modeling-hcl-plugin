# Workshop logistics

## Invitation

Send the invitation one week before the session. Name the domain, the date, the length, and the place or the link. Ask people to think about the workflows, the important changes of state, and their open questions. Tell them that no knowledge of event modeling is necessary.

## Room

- Use a wide wall, cards, markers, and a visible timer.
- People stand, so that everyone can see the wall and add to it.
- Prepare empty card templates for events, commands, read models, screens, and given, when, then scenarios.

## Remote setup

- Use a shared drawing tool, a video call, and a shared notes document. Everyone can edit.
- Turn on the recording only when the group agrees.
- Prepare the same card templates as in a room.
- Test the tools with one participant before the session.

Remote sessions are easier to record and to share. They make drawing and side talks harder. The remote format rules of the skill handle this.

## A plan over several days

For a large domain, split the work over three days. Keep the order of the gates.

1. Day 1, four hours: roles, events, chapters, story order, and screens.
2. Day 2, four hours: streams, lifecycles, commands, automations, external mappings, and read models.
3. Day 3, three hours: command scenarios, read model scenarios, the question tour of the reviews, slice status, ownership, and next steps.

Start each day with the first gate that still fails. Write the chapter summaries after the last day, in the follow-up.

## A repair session

A weak workshop shows these signs in the follow-up summary: decisions without a reason, many open questions on one part, a participant with no statement, or a part that ran out of time. Plan a short repair session for that part. Invite the people who can answer its open questions.
